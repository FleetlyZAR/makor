**GUIDE** Notice the two trees in the middle. They are placed carefully, with the man, the woman and the command, so that the next chapter can test each one.  <sub>[study.context.literary] m019</sub>

## 4:01  To cultivate and keep
*Picture:* The man, small in the landscape, at work among the trees of the garden, tending the ground at first light.
<sub>Genesis 2:15</sub>  **READER** Then the LORD God took the man and placed him in the Garden of Eden to cultivate and keep it.  <sub>m027</sub>
**GUIDE** Cultivate and keep. The first verb means to work or serve, and is the ordinary word for serving God in worship. The second means to guard and keep, the word for keeping God's commands.  <sub>[study.originalLanguages] m028</sub>
**GUIDE** Together they describe the Levites in the tabernacle. Adam's work is farming, but it is also priestly service in the place where God dwells.  <sub>[study.originalLanguages] m029</sub>
*Picture:* Stillness in the garden; the tree of the knowledge of good and evil standing at the centre; the voice comes with no figure, only wind moving softly through the leaves.
<sub>Genesis 2:16</sub>  **READER** And the LORD God commanded him,  **GOD** “You may eat freely from every tree of the garden,  <sub>m030 m031</sub>
<sub>Genesis 2:17</sub>  **GOD** but you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil; for in the day that you eat of it, you will surely die.”  <sub>m032</sub>
**GUIDE** Listen to the order. The command opens with permission, every tree of the garden, and only then sets one limit. God's commands are not the grudging rules of a jealous master, but the boundaries of a good home.  <sub>[study.god] m033</sub>
**GUIDE** And the warning is plain. God does not hide the consequences of disobedience. His word defines what is good and what is evil, and life depends on trusting it.  <sub>[study.god] m034</sub>
**GUIDE** Whatever the knowledge of good and evil means exactly, the heart of the matter is trust: whether the man will receive it from God's word, or seize it on his own terms.  <sub>[study.hermeneutics.debates[1].bearing] m035</sub>

*Picture:* Dusk deepening into night over the garden; the man asleep beneath a tree, seen from far away; soft light gathering near him. No figure of God.

<sub>Genesis 2:22</sub>  **READER** And from the rib that the LORD God had taken from the man, He made a woman and brought her to him.  <sub>m046</sub>
**GUIDE** The deep sleep is the same sleep that falls on Abram before God makes covenant with him. God works while the man is passive.  <sub>[study.originalLanguages] m047</sub>
**GUIDE** And the verb for made is built. God builds the woman as a builder raises a house, and then He Himself brings her to the man.  <sub>[study.originalLanguages] m048</sub>
*Picture:* Morning light. Far across the garden, two small figures meeting among the trees; nothing close, nothing detailed.
<sub>Genesis 2:23</sub>  **READER** And the man said: “This is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called ‘woman,’ for out of man she was taken.”  <sub>m049</sub>
**GUIDE** These are the first recorded human words in the Bible, and they are poetry. Man and woman, ish and ishshah, sound alike. Bone of my bones is the language of family.  <sub>[study.originalLanguages] m050</sub>
<sub>Genesis 2:24</sub>  **READER** For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and they will become one flesh.  <sub>m051</sub>
**GUIDE** The narrator steps out of the story to apply it to every marriage after it. To be united is to cling or hold fast, the word Ruth uses of Naomi. Marriage is a loyalty chosen and kept, not only a feeling.  <sub>[study.originalLanguages] m052</sub>
*Picture:* The garden in full light, the two far off together among the trees; peace, no fear.
<sub>Genesis 2:25</sub>  **READER** And the man and his wife were both naked, and they were not ashamed.  <sub>m053</sub>
**GUIDE** Naked, and not ashamed. The line closes the scene and sets up the next one. Every gift in this chapter is about to be tested.  <sub>[study.context.literary] m054</sub>

## 8:16  The last Adam

**GUIDE** Paul quotes this chapter and names Jesus as the last Adam.  <sub>[study.christ] m055</sub>

**GUIDE** Adam was placed in a garden to serve and guard it, and given one word to keep. He failed at every point. Christ comes as the obedient Man who serves His Father perfectly.  <sub>[study.christ] m057</sub>

## 9:24  Eden restored
*Picture:* The garden seen from far above; then thorns and toil; then a radiant city with a river flowing through it and trees on both banks.
**GUIDE** When chapter three comes, each gift in this garden is damaged. The ground is cursed, work becomes toil, the marriage is strained, shame replaces nakedness without shame, and the way to the tree of life is barred.  <sub>[study.oneStory] m065</sub>
**GUIDE** But the Bible ends with Eden restored and surpassed. The river of the water of life flows from the throne of God and of the Lamb, and the tree of life stands on either side of it.  <sub>[study.oneStory] m066</sub>
**READER** eat from the tree of life in the Paradise of God,  <sub>(Revelation 2:7, quoted verbatim from the study) m067</sub>
**GUIDE** The garden has become a city, but it is still a home where God and His people live together.  <sub>[study.oneStory] m068</sub>
