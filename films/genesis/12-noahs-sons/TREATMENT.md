## The idea

The Covenant with Noah ended with Noah pressing a vine cutting into the ground and the line "what happens in his
tent will divide his sons". This film pays that off: the first vintage, the lamp in the tent, the cloak, and the
only words Noah ever speaks.

It is a hard passage for a family film. The film handles it the way the narrator does: with restraint. Noah's
nakedness is never shown. The covering is told slowly, the brothers' care against Ham's looking, and the camera
looks away with them.

Four ideas carry it:

1. **The flood cleaned the world, not the heart.** The righteous man who walked with God falls within a few verses
   of the covenant, and the scene replays the garden: a man of the ground plants, the fruit is taken, nakedness is
   exposed, a garment covers it, a knowing follows, then curse and blessing. Chalal, "began", opens each new start
   in these chapters that goes wrong (6:1, 10:8, 11:6). A new humanity needs a new head.
2. **Expose or cover.** Ham saw and told; Shem and Japheth took the garment, walked backward and did not see. The
   fifth commandment, honour without excusing, and love that covers.
3. **Canaan, not Ham, and never a race.** The oracle is prophecy about the Canaanites Israel would meet, whose
   judgment waited until their own iniquity was complete (15:16). The film says plainly that the "curse of Ham"
   used to defend the enslavement of Africans is a lie the text gives no ground for: Ham is not cursed, and Cush,
   Mizraim and Put are not cursed at all.
4. **The God of Shem.** Noah blesses the LORD, not Shem. Shem means name, and the play runs on to Babel ("make a
   name for ourselves") and Abram ("I will make your name great"), to Luke's "the son of Shem", to the Word who
   pitched His tent among us, and to the nations brought in. Christ, the last Adam, does not look away from our
   shame; He bears it, scorning its shame, and clothes His people. Rahab and the Canaanite woman show the curse is
   not the last word.

The brick-maker is the first hearer three times, all from the study: at Sinai hearing the fifth commandment with
his own old father beside him (crossReferences[5]); on the plains across the Jordan looking at the land of Canaan,
with God's warning that Israel held no immunity (crossReferences[2]); and the close, laying his cloak over his
sleeping father (hermeneutics.authorIntent, questions[1]).

## The shape

1. **Cold open (flash-forward):** Noah's vine from the last film, years later a vineyard; the first vintage; a fast
   montage (the cup by lamplight, Noah's face, the tent flap falling, the spilled cup, Ham at the door from behind,
   the brothers walking backward with the cloak); Noah waking under the cloak; READER 9:25 "Cursed be Canaan!";
   black. The hook at 0:17, the picture following each question: "Why does the man who survived the flood end up
   drunk in his tent? What did Ham actually do? Why is Canaan cursed, when Ham did the wrong? And how was this
   chapter twisted to defend slavery? Four questions. Twelve verses." Title at 0:35.
2. **The three sons (9:18 to 19):** one family, one skin; the whole earth from them; the narrator's first flag,
   "Ham was the father of Canaan"; Canaan a boy seen from behind.
3. **A man of the soil (9:20), SET PIECE 1, the first vintage:** ish ha'adamah, adamah, Lamech's hope (Genesis
   5:29); chalal and the beginnings that go wrong (6:1, 10:8, 11:6); the harvest and the press; wine that gladdens
   (Psalm 104:15) and wine the mocker (Proverbs 20:1); the text neither excuses nor lectures.
4. **Inside his tent (9:21 to 22):** question one answered (walked with God, Genesis 6:9; every inclination evil,
   Genesis 8:21 in the GOD voice); the garden replayed (pictures from The Fall); galah, he exposed himself; Ham at
   the door from behind, then telling his brothers; ervah and the narrator's second flag; question two answered
   with debate one in brief and its bearing; Habakkuk 2:15.
5. **Walking backward (9:23), SET PIECE 2, the covering:** slow, step by step, faces turned away, nothing of Noah
   seen; the narrator's slow telling, nakedness three times, "the garment"; the brick-maker at Sinai, Exodus 20:12
   in the GOD voice, Deuteronomy 27:16; what carries forward (Ephesians 5:18; honour; love covers).
6. **Cursed be Canaan (9:24 to 25):** Noah waking; his only words; ebed abadim, the superlative; question three
   answered with debate two and its bearing; the brick-maker across the Jordan, Leviticus 18 in GUIDE words; Genesis
   15:16 in the GOD voice; the Gibeonites, woodcutters and water carriers (Joshua 9:23).
7. **A lie about Ham:** question four answered from descriptionVsPrescription: the curse of Ham used to justify the
   enslavement of Africans; Ham not cursed, Canaan is; Cush, Mizraim and Put not cursed; prophecy not command; the
   translation note that servant of servants is no comment on any people's worth; one family. A gold graphic of
   the sons of Ham with only Canaan marked.
8. **The God of Shem (9:26 to 27):** barak; the LORD only with Shem; Shem means name, Genesis 11:4 and 12:2 (GOD
   voice); yapht and yepheth; debate three (who dwells in Shem's tents) and its bearing.
9. **And then he died (9:28 to 29), SET PIECE 3, the three sons go out:** 950 years; the Genesis 5 refrain and
   Noah's entry closed; God sends no flood though the family sinned; the three families leaving the mountain in
   three directions; God sovereign over history.
10. **The faithful Son (Christ):** the last Adam (1 Corinthians 15:45) and the faithful Son (John 8:29); the robe
    divided at the foot of the posts, Hebrews 12:2, Galatians 3:27; Luke 3:36, John 1:14 (the tent), Ephesians
    2:19; Galatians 3:13, Rahab and the Canaanite woman; the caution against reading the wine as the Supper; the
    nations in white, Revelation 22:3.
11. **The cloak at evening (close and open loop):** the brick-maker laying his cloak over his sleeping father;
    questions[1]; the empty mountain at dawn with three paths away from it; "Next, Genesis names their children,
    nation by nation. And every one of them is family." End card, WATCH NEXT The Table of Nations.

## Decisions for Luyanda (as proposed)

- **D1, Noah's nakedness is never shown.** The tent from outside, the lamp dimming, the spilled cup, Ham from
  behind in the doorway with nothing inside in view; the brothers framed at the shoulders, the cloak lowering out
  of the frame; Noah only seen awake under the cloak. Every keyframe and clip of the tent checked for this.
  Fallback: the covering shown only as the brothers' turned faces and the cloak in their hands.
- **D2, Ham's sin as the text tells it.** On screen: he looks and he tells. The sexual reading of the idiom is
  heard in one modest line, with the study's bearing after it; nothing of it is shown. Fallback: leave the second
  view out of the narration (the study's debate would then be heard only in part).
- **D3, one family, one skin, and the lie named.** Ham has the same skin and hair as his brothers in every shot. A
  short chapter says plainly that the "curse of Ham" used to defend the enslavement of Africans is a misuse the
  text gives no ground for, from the study's descriptionVsPrescription and translation note. Pictures: an old book
  by candlelight and a pair of iron shackles lying open and empty on planks by the sea; no slave ship, no people,
  no suffering shown; then the gold graphic of Ham's sons with only Canaan marked. Fallback: the graphic only.
- **D4, Canaan as a boy (ours).** The text names Canaan as Ham's son in 9:18 but does not say he was born yet or
  present. The film shows him as a small boy, always from behind, and grown among Ham's family as they leave.
  Fallback: Canaan never shown, named only on the graphic.
- **D5, the wine without comedy.** The vintage is a joy (Psalm 104:15). Drunkenness is one unsteady rise, the tent
  flap falling and the spilled cup: no stumbling, no slurring, no caricature. Grape treaders wear long robes held
  clear of the juice and below the knee (memory: no shorts). Fallback: no drinking shown, only the cup.
- **D6, God's voice from elsewhere.** Nothing in 9:18 to 29 is God's speech; Noah's oracle is read by the READER
  (a human speaker; `SPEAKERS` keeps "he said," from going to GOD). The GOD voice reads five words of God quoted
  from elsewhere: Genesis 11:6, 8:21, 15:16 and 12:2, and Exodus 20:12. Fallback: the READER reads them all.
- **D7, Christ.** Jesus from straight behind on a road at dusk, warm olive brown skin and thick black curly hair,
  no part of the face; at the cross only the three posts far off with no figures and soldiers' hands casting lots
  over the robe; the Canaanite woman from behind with only the hem and sandalled feet before her; the crowd in
  white from behind with no throne and no figure above. Fallback: the robe on the stones only.
- **D8, steps past the study, each flagged in its source field.**
  1. The hook's four questions are ours; each answer is the study's (oneStory and authorIntent; debates[0];
     debates[1]; descriptionVsPrescription).
  2. The brick-maker at Sinai with his old father, across the Jordan looking at Canaan, and laying his cloak over
     his father: the series' own first hearer, from the study's crossReferences and authorIntent.
  3. The vintage and the press, Noah's hand on Shem's head at the blessing, the cairn, and the three families
     leaving in three directions: the text gives the events or the words, not these scenes. The cairn is ours (the
     text gives no burial).
  4. "Love covers a multitude of sins" is the study's own summary of 1 Peter 4:8 and Proverbs 10:12, spoken by the
     GUIDE and not quoted as Scripture. The Canaanite woman (Matthew 15:22 to 28) and Rahab are told in the GUIDE's
     words from the study's christ and god fields, not quoted.
  5. The open loop names Genesis 10 ahead of its own film.
- **D9, length.** 19:20 estimated, level with The Covenant with Noah. All three debates are heard in brief with
  their bearing. Cut for length while drafting: Isaiah 61:10 and Ephesians 6:2 (both quoted in the study, the
  first worth putting back if Luyanda wants ten seconds more). Also left out: Genesis 19:32 to 35 (Lot's
  daughters), Lamentations 4:21, Exodus 21:15 and 17 as quotations, Proverbs 30:17, Leviticus 18:28 as a quotation,
  Judges 1:28, 1 Kings 9:20 to 21, Revelation 3:18 and 7:9, Ephesians 2:13 and 2:14, Romans 11:17, Galatians 6:1,
  Matthew 1:5, Exodus 3:6, Mark 15:24.
- **D10, Noah's age and look.** The older portrait throughout; at 950 the same man with white hair, close only once
  at his tent door, then from behind. His sons grey at the cairn, from behind. Fallback: no aged shots; the lamp
  going out stands for his death.
- **D11, working title.** The hook pays off a title on the curse. Proposed for checking in vidIQ at the YouTube
  pack: "The Curse of Ham Is a Lie: What Genesis 9 Really Says", or "Why Did Noah Curse His Grandson? Genesis 9
  Explained". Fallback: decide at the YouTube pack, as before.
